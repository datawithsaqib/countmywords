How characters are counted

Characters are counted as visible characters. An emoji such as 😀 counts as one character, not two, and combined characters and flags are counted the same way. “Characters” includes spaces, line breaks and punctuation. “Chars, no spaces” counts the same text with all spaces removed.

For example, “Hello 😀” is 7 characters including the space and 6 characters without it.

How to use this character counter without spaces

This character counter without spaces shows you two numbers at once: characters including spaces, and characters without spaces. Paste your text into the box above and both numbers update as you type. The first number is the full length of your text. The second number counts the same text with all spaces removed. Use the first number when a form or a website counts spaces, and the second when it does not.

That difference matters more than it looks. A short sentence can have many spaces, and those spaces count in some places and not in others. For example, a post with 20 words will have about 19 spaces. If the limit is 200 characters, those spaces use up almost a tenth of the limit. Checking both numbers helps you avoid a surprise when you paste your text into another website.

Characters with spaces and characters without spaces

Characters include letters, numbers, punctuation, spaces and line breaks. When you count characters with spaces, every space and every line break is counted. When you count characters without spaces, only the visible letters, numbers and punctuation are counted. Emoji are counted as one visible character each, so “Hello 😀” is 7 characters including the space and 6 characters without it.

Use the “with spaces” number for most online forms, social media posts and search descriptions, because those systems usually count the whole text. Use the “without spaces” number when a teacher, an editor or a website asks for it clearly, or when you want to know how much real content you have written, without counting the gaps between words.

Character limits you may meet

Different places use different limits. A text message is often limited to 160 characters in one message. A search result description is usually shown at around 150 to 160 characters, so a longer description may be cut off. A headline or a title works best when it is short enough to read at a glance. Always check the limit for the place where you will publish your text, because limits change and they are not the same everywhere.

If your text is too long, try to cut whole words rather than single letters. Remove a word that adds little, shorten a long phrase, or split the text into two shorter parts. If you cut letters from the middle of words, the text becomes hard to read, even if it fits the limit.

A character counter for Twitter 280

A character counter for Twitter, now called X, is useful because a single post is limited to 280 characters. That limit includes letters, numbers, spaces, punctuation and emoji. A link in a post also uses up characters, so count the whole post, link included, before you publish it.

To fit 280 characters, write your main point first, then cut the rest. Remove extra words such as “very” and “really”, use short words where you can, and avoid repeating the same idea. If your point does not fit, split it into two posts, and make the first post complete on its own, so readers understand it even if they do not see the second one.

Check the count before you post, not after. Paste your draft into the box above, read the character count, and edit until it fits. That is faster than writing a post, finding it is too long, and then trying to shorten it in the small posting box.

When to check both numbers

Check both numbers when you are not sure which one a website uses. If the form says “characters” with no extra words, it usually means characters including spaces. If it says “characters excluding spaces”, use the second number. If you are writing for school, ask your teacher which number counts. A quick check here takes a few seconds, and it can save you from cutting a good paragraph by mistake, or from sending a text that is too long for the form.
