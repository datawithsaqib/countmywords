How sentences are counted

A sentence ends with a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark. Decimal numbers such as 3.14 are not split into two sentences, and blank lines are not counted as sentences. Empty text has 0 sentences, and text with words but no end mark counts as 1 sentence.

For example, “Hello world. How are you? I am fine!” is 8 words and 3 sentences.

How to use this sentence counter online

This sentence counter online counts the sentences in your text as you type. Paste your essay, article or report into the box above, and the sentence count updates at once. You can also see the word count, the character count and the paragraph count on the same page. There is no sign-up, and your text stays on your device.

Use the sentence count to check the shape of your writing, not just the length. A text with 1,000 words and only 20 sentences has very long sentences, at about 50 words each. A text with 1,000 words and 100 sentences has very short sentences, at about 10 words each. Both can be hard to read. Most clear writing mixes short and long sentences, so the reader gets a change of pace.

What is a good sentence length?

There is no single correct length, but a useful guide is to aim for an average of about 15 to 20 words per sentence in most essays and articles. Short sentences of 5 to 10 words are good for a clear point or a warning. Long sentences of 25 to 30 words can work for a complex idea, but they are easy to lose track of. If a sentence is longer than 35 words, try to split it into two sentences, or remove a phrase that adds little.

Read your sentences aloud. If you run out of breath before the end, the sentence is probably too long. If every sentence sounds the same, try to vary the length. A short sentence after a long one can make the main point stand out. Varying the length is more useful than trying to make every sentence the same length.

Paragraph counter: how paragraphs are counted

This page is also a paragraph counter. A paragraph is a block of text separated from the next block by a blank line. Empty lines are not counted as paragraphs, and a single block of text with no blank line counts as 1 paragraph, even if it is long. The paragraph count helps you see if your text is broken up well.

A good paragraph usually covers one main idea. In an essay, a paragraph might be 100 to 200 words, but that is a guide, not a rule. A very long paragraph can hide several ideas. A very short paragraph can look unfinished. If a paragraph covers two different ideas, split it. If two short paragraphs cover the same idea, join them.

To check your paragraphs, look at the first sentence of each one. It should tell the reader what that paragraph is about. If you cannot say what a paragraph is about in one short sentence, the paragraph may need to be rewritten or split.

How to check your writing with this tool

First, paste your full draft and read the sentence, word and paragraph counts together. Divide the word count by the sentence count to find your average sentence length. Then look for the longest sentences. You can find them by reading through the text and marking any sentence that takes more than two lines.

Second, check the balance. If most sentences are long, shorten a few. If most are short, join a few short sentences into a longer one where the ideas belong together. Third, check the paragraph count. If you have a 1,000-word essay with only two paragraphs, split it. If you have 20 paragraphs for the same length, join some of them.

Finally, remember that counting sentences does not check grammar or meaning. A sentence can be counted correctly and still be unclear. Use the count as a guide, then read the text yourself, or ask someone else to read it, before you hand it in.
