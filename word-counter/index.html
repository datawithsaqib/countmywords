How words are counted

Words are groups of letters and numbers. A word with an apostrophe, such as don’t, counts as one word, and a hyphenated word, such as well-known, also counts as one word. Numbers count as words, and text in other languages counts too. Extra spaces, line breaks and blank lines do not create extra words.

For example, “Hello world!” is 2 words, 12 characters including the space, and 1 sentence.

How other languages are counted

This counter uses your browser’s built-in word segmentation where it is available. That handles English and other space-separated languages, and it also splits Chinese and Japanese text into word units — for example, 你好世界 counts as 2 words (你好 + 世界), not 1 word and not 4 characters. That count is approximate: no word counter is exact for every language, and Chinese and Japanese have no spaces to mark word boundaries. In older browsers without word segmentation, a run of Chinese or Japanese characters counts as 1 word. Accented European letters, Turkish letters such as İ and ı, and Arabic script are counted as letters, the same as English letters. A decimal such as 3.14 counts as one word, and a number with commas such as 1,000 also counts as one word.

How to use this word counter for essays

A word counter for essays is a simple check you can use before you hand in your work. Paste your essay into the box above, or type it there directly. The word count updates as you type, so you can see at once if you are under or over the limit. When you are done, use the Copy button to copy the text, or Clear to start again. There is no sign-up, and your text stays on your device.

Start by pasting a full draft, not just one paragraph. That way the count includes your introduction, your main points and your conclusion. If your teacher gave you a word limit, compare the number at the top with that limit before you edit. It is easier to cut a long draft down than to add words to a short one at the end.

Check the other numbers too. The character count helps if your essay is pasted into an online form with a limit. The sentence and paragraph counts help you see the shape of your work. If you have only two very long paragraphs, your ideas may be packed too tightly. If you have many very short paragraphs, your argument may be broken up too much.

Word limits for essays and assignments

Word limits are set by your teacher or your school, so always follow the limit you were given. The numbers below are common examples, not rules for every class. A short school essay is often about 500 words. A longer school essay is often 800 to 1,000 words. A college essay or a short assignment is often 1,000 to 1,500 words. A longer assignment or a report may be 2,000 to 3,000 words. If your limit is different, use the number you were given.

Most limits allow a small amount of give and take, but do not rely on that. Some teachers count the words in the main text only, and do not count the title, the list of sources, or a table. Others count everything. If you are not sure, ask your teacher which parts count. Then check your draft with this tool and keep the main text inside the limit.

How many pages is 1000 words?

This is one of the most common questions about essay length, and the answer depends on spacing. As a rough guide, 1,000 words is about 2 pages if the text is single-spaced, and about 4 pages if it is double-spaced. The same guide works for other lengths: 500 words is about 1 page single-spaced or 2 pages double-spaced, and 2,000 words is about 4 pages single-spaced or 8 pages double-spaced.

Font size, margins and line spacing change the result, so these are estimates only. A larger font or wider margins will make the same 1,000 words fill more pages. A smaller font will make it fill fewer pages. When a teacher asks for a word count, use the word count, not the page count, because the word count does not change when you change the font.

Why count words, and how to stay within the limit

Counting words helps you plan. If your limit is 1,000 words and you have three main points, you can plan about 300 words for each point and about 100 words for the introduction and conclusion together. That plan is easier to follow than writing without a target and then trying to cut the text down.

If you are over the limit, look for repeated ideas, long examples that say the same thing twice, and sentences that could be shorter. If you are under the limit, look for a point that needs more evidence or a clearer example. Do not add filler sentences just to reach the number. A clear essay that is a little short is better than a long essay that repeats itself.

A word counter with no sign-up is useful here because you can check your work quickly, as often as you like, without creating an account or uploading your essay to a website. Paste the text, check the number, edit, and check again. That is the whole process, and it takes less than a minute. In short: open the page, paste your essay, and read the count — no sign-up needed.

Worked examples: word goals, unique words and platform limits

Example 1 — a 500-word essay goal. Choose the 500 goal above and paste a draft of 430 words. The progress bar shows 86%, and the text reads “430 / 500 words (86%) — Below the safe zone — add about 20 words to reach it.” The safe zone is 450–550 words, which is the goal plus or minus 10%. At 430 words you are 20 words short of the bottom of that zone, so one short paragraph or two or three sentences of evidence will bring you inside it. At 560 words the same goal would read “Above the safe zone — cut about 10 words”, because the top of the zone is 550. The bar turns red only when you pass the top of the safe zone, not when you pass the goal itself, because being a few words over the exact goal is normal.

Example 2 — unique words and average word length. Paste “The cat sat on the mat. The cat was happy.” The counter shows 10 words and 7 unique words (“the” appears three times and “cat” twice, so 10 words minus 3 repeats is 7), and an average word length of 3.1 characters. Now paste the same idea written differently: “The cat sat on the mat, content and sleepy.” That is 9 words, 9 unique words, and an average word length of 3.9 characters. The unique-word count does not measure quality — a text can repeat words well — but a sudden drop in unique words across a long essay is a sign that the same few words are doing all the work. Check it paragraph by paragraph rather than once for the whole essay.

Example 3 — does it fit on X? Paste a draft post of 320 characters. The X bar shows “320 / 280 — over by 40”, so the post does not fit as one post on X. Cut 40 characters — for example, remove the word “really” (6 letters plus a space) and shorten “because” to “as” — and the bar moves under the limit. The same 320 characters are a small part of an Instagram caption (2,200) and a tiny part of a LinkedIn post (3,000), so the answer depends on where the text is going, not only on how long it is. A meta description of 320 characters is far too long: search results usually show only about the first 155–160 characters, so the second half would be cut off. The SMS bar shows the same 320 characters as two messages of 160 characters each.

Example 4 — the 650-word college essay. Many college applications ask for about 650 words, which is why 650 is one of the goal presets. A 650-word essay is about 1 page single-spaced or 2 to 3 pages double-spaced, takes about 3 minutes 15 seconds to read at 200 words per minute, and about 5 minutes to speak aloud. Plan it as an introduction of about 80 words, three main points of about 150 words each, and a conclusion of about 120 words — that adds up to 650. If your draft is 700 words, you are inside the 585–715 safe zone, so do not cut good sentences just to hit 650 exactly.

What counts as a unique word

A unique word is a word that appears at least once, counted once no matter how often it appears. Capital letters do not make a new word: “The” and “the” are the same word. Contractions count as one word, so “don’t” is one unique word, and hyphenated words such as “well-known” are one word as well. Numbers count as words, so “2026” is a unique word the first time it appears. The average word length is the average length of your words in visible characters — for most English writing it is between 4 and 5 characters, and a much higher average usually means long technical words or long names, not better writing.
