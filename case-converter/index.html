What each style does

  • UPPERCASE — every letter becomes a capital letter.
  • lowercase — every letter becomes a small letter.
  • Title Case — the first letter of each word becomes a capital.
  • Sentence case — the first letter of each sentence becomes a capital, and the rest become small letters.
  • camelCase — words are joined with no spaces, and each word after the first starts with a capital: helloWorld.
  • PascalCase — like camelCase, but the first word also starts with a capital: HelloWorld.
  • snake_case — words are joined with underscores: hello_world.
  • kebab-case — words are joined with hyphens: hello-world.

Conversion keeps your punctuation and spacing where the style allows it. For camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case and kebab-case, spaces and punctuation are replaced by the style’s joining rule, because that is what those styles mean. An apostrophe inside a word is removed rather than splitting the word, so don’t becomes dontStop in camelCase and dont_stop in snake_case. Uppercase and lowercase use your browser’s locale, so Turkish dotted and dotless I and Greek letters follow the rules of that locale. Names with an internal capital, such as McDonald or iPhone, are split at that capital, so snake_case gives mc_donald and i_phone — check names before using them as code or file names.

When to use each style

UPPERCASE is useful for short labels, warnings and headings that need to stand out. It is hard to read in long text, so use it for a few words, not for a whole page. Lowercase is useful for fixing text that was typed in capitals, and for places that expect small letters only.

Title Case is the usual style for headings and book titles. The first letter of each main word becomes a capital. Sentence case is the usual style for normal sentences: the first letter is a capital, and the rest are small letters unless a word is a name. Use sentence case for most writing, and Title Case for titles.

CamelCase joins words with no spaces, and each word after the first starts with a capital, such as helloWorld. PascalCase is the same, but the first word also starts with a capital, such as HelloWorld. Both are common in programming, where spaces are not allowed in names. Snake_case joins words with underscores, such as hello_world, and kebab-case joins words with hyphens, such as hello-world. Snake_case is common in file names and in some programming languages. Kebab-case is common in web addresses and file names, because hyphens are easy to read in a link.

For long text, Title Case is not the right choice. It makes a paragraph hard to read. Use Title Case for titles only, and Sentence case for the text under the title.

Tips and limits

Conversion keeps your punctuation and spacing where the style allows it. For camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case and kebab-case, spaces and punctuation are replaced by the joining rule of that style, because that is what those styles mean. Numbers and names in other languages are handled as letters and numbers, but check the result if your text uses a script other than Latin letters. Always read the converted text once before you paste it into your work, especially if it is a file name or a piece of code, because a small change in a name can break a link or a program.
