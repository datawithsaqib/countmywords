How the estimates work

These are estimates, not exact predictions. Reading time is based on about 200 words per minute, and speaking time on about 130 words per minute, which is a normal speaking speed. Real times vary with the text, the topic and the reader — for example, a 1,000-word article takes about 5 minutes to read and about 7–8 minutes to speak aloud.

How to use this reading time calculator

This reading time calculator estimates how long your text takes to read or to speak aloud. Paste your text into the box above and the estimates update live. The total word count is shown at the top, because the estimates are based on that count. There is no sign-up, and your text stays on your device.

Use the reading time when you want to know if an article, a lesson or a set of notes is the right length. Use the speaking time when you are planning a talk, a lesson, a presentation or a video script. If the speaking time is longer than the time you have, you need to cut the text or speak faster — and cutting the text is usually the better choice.

How the reading time is estimated

The reading time is an estimate based on about 200 words per minute. That is a normal reading speed for most adults reading ordinary text. The speaking time is based on about 130 words per minute, which is a normal speaking speed. These are estimates, not exact predictions. Real times vary with the text, the topic and the reader.

A simple text with short words is faster to read than a difficult text with long words and long sentences. A reader who knows the topic well will read faster than a reader who is learning it for the first time. A speaker who pauses for effect, or who waits for questions, will take longer than the estimate. Use the numbers as a guide, and add a little extra time if the text is difficult or if you plan to pause.

As a rough guide, 500 words takes about 2 to 3 minutes to read and about 4 minutes to speak aloud. 1,000 words takes about 5 minutes to read and about 7 to 8 minutes to speak aloud. 2,000 words takes about 10 minutes to read and about 15 minutes to speak aloud. These are the same speeds the calculator uses, worked out for common lengths.

Speaking time calculator: planning a talk

A speaking time calculator is useful when you have a fixed time for a talk. If you have 10 minutes, plan for about 1,300 words at 130 words per minute. That leaves a little time for pauses and for a short introduction. If you write 2,000 words for a 10-minute talk, you will have to rush, and the audience will find it hard to follow.

Write your talk in the words you would actually say, not in the words you would write in an essay. Spoken sentences are usually shorter, and spoken language uses simpler words. Then paste the talk into the calculator and check the speaking time. Read it aloud once with a timer if you can. That test will tell you more than the estimate alone, because your own speaking speed may be faster or slower than 130 words per minute.

For a lesson or a video, the same idea applies. Plan the main points first, then write the script, then check the time. If the script is too long, cut a whole point rather than speaking faster. A clear talk that finishes on time is better than a rushed talk that tries to cover too much.

Tips and limits

Do not use the reading time for a single sentence or a very short text. The estimate is most useful for texts of a few hundred words or more. For short texts, the time is less than a minute, and the difference between reading and speaking is small. Also, do not treat the estimate as a promise. If you need an exact time, read the text aloud with a timer. The calculator gives you a quick plan; the timer gives you the real time.
